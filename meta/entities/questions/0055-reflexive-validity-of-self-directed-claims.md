# Which self-claims can the project's own graph support?

## Summary

This project studies the Science toolkit using the Science toolkit. Its evidence about
the tool is gathered, structured, aggregated, and believed through the very machinery
whose adequacy is in question. That is a closed loop, and it is the project's structural
condition rather than an incidental feature.

The question: **which claims about Science can Science's own graph legitimately support,
and which require an instrument outside the system?**

Surfaced during the 2026-07-25 `explore-ideas` triage rather than by a lens agent: the blind pass could not reach it, because the lens agents had no view of what the project already asks.

## Why It Matters

- Determines whether any belief the graph reports about its own design counts as evidence
  or as restatement. A tool that scores itself well on its own instrument has demonstrated
  internal consistency, not validity.
- Decides where an external instrument is mandatory — plausibly the tool-evaluation
  questions (`question:0041`, `question:0042`, `hypothesis:0008`), which is why those
  were framed around human participants and external benchmarks rather than graph state.
- Risk if unanswered: the project accumulates well-supported beliefs about itself that no
  outside observer would accept, and cannot tell which of its conclusions those are.

## Current Evidence

- The circularity is real and pervasive: paper summaries, evidence payloads, syntheses,
  and attention rankings informing design decisions were themselves produced by the
  agent-driven workflows under evaluation.
- `question:0017` already reaches for external grounding via benchmark portfolios, and
  names leakage — evidence and ground truth drawn from the same source — as its central
  risk. That is this question in miniature, scoped to benchmarks.
- The project has partial defences (D-003's bounded beliefs, artifact-guarded review) but
  none of them distinguish self-referential support from independent support.

## Thoughts

- Best current interpretation: a claim about the toolkit is externally supportable only
  when its evidence could have been produced without the toolkit. That is a sharp,
  checkable criterion and would partition the existing graph immediately.
- Major remaining uncertainty: whether the partition leaves enough behind. If nearly all
  current evidence about Science is Science-produced, the honest conclusion is that the
  project's self-directed beliefs are weaker than their recorded strength suggests.

## Connections to Project

- Related hypotheses: `hypothesis:0007-working-model`
- Required analyses: apply the "could this have been produced without the toolkit"
  criterion to existing evidence lines on tool-design propositions.
- Priority level: high — it conditions the interpretation of every self-directed claim.
