# Nexus: Correlation Discovery over Collections of Spatio-Temporal Tabular Data

- **Authors:** Yue Gong, Sainyam Galhotra, and Raul Castro Fernandez
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal/Venue:** Proceedings of the ACM on Management of Data / SIGMOD
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3654957
- **BibTeX key:** Gong2024
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

Gong et al. introduce Nexus, an end-to-end system for discovering interesting correlations across collections of spatio-temporal tabular datasets [@Gong2024].
The system is designed for exploratory causal analysis: it helps users find correlated variables and candidate confounders before causal claims are established.

## Methods

Nexus aligns tabular datasets across space and time, handles missing data during correlation computation, builds correlation graphs, and highlights structured correlations that may be useful for hypothesis generation.
It evaluates performance on Chicago Open Data and United Nations datasets.

## Key Findings

The paper reports that Nexus can surface large numbers of correlations and distill interesting structures, including examples supported by social-science literature [@Gong2024].
It emphasizes that correlation discovery helps cast a wide net, but downstream causal analysis still requires assumptions and domain expertise.

## Relevance

Science needs a clear artifact type for exploratory correlation discovery.
Correlation graphs can propose hypotheses, suggest confounders, and identify data to gather, but they should not strengthen causal propositions directly.
The alignment and missingness pipeline is evidence provenance.

## Limitations

Correlation is not causation.
Interestingness is partly subjective and user-goal dependent.
Spatio-temporal aggregation can introduce ecological bias or alignment artifacts.
