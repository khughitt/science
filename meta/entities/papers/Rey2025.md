# Beyond Mechanisms, Models, and Robustness: When Explanatory Success Entitles Persistence Claims

- **Authors:** Philip Rey
- **Year:** 2025
- **Venue:** Working paper (PhilArchive / Zenodo)

## Key Contribution

Rey introduces Active Memory Engineering (AME) as a fourth meta-methodological constraint
alongside the three standard pillars of contemporary philosophy of science — mechanistic
explanation, agent-based modeling (ABM), and robustness analysis. AME is an *admissibility
check* rather than a competing explanatory framework: it specifies the conditions under which
explanatory success in open, dissipative, non-Markovian systems also licenses *persistence
claims* — claims that a target system can continue to exist and function over time. The paper's
central diagnosis is that the standard triad all presuppose persistence as a background condition
rather than explaining it, creating a systematic "isolation bias." AME fills this gap by evaluating
persistence at the level of trajectories ("film") rather than snapshots, through three coupled
audit dimensions: alignment, memory utilization, and stability under temporally correlated
perturbations.

## Methods

This is a philosophical/methodological paper. There are no empirical data or experiments. The
argument proceeds through:

1. **Analytical survey of the three pillars** (§§2.1–2.5): mechanistic explanation is synchronic
   and presupposes mechanism identity; ABM treats agent persistence as a built-in modelling
   setup; robustness analysis tests stability of descriptions under model/parameter perturbation
   but not stability of described systems under realistic open-system dynamics. All three inherit
   an isolation bias: they treat environmental coupling as noise or boundary conditions rather
   than as a structured dynamical partner.

2. **Introduction of AME as a meta-methodological audit** (§§3–4): AME is formulated around
   three operational criteria — (i) *alignment* (temporal-environmental synchronization; phase
   coherence across relevant lags), (ii) *memory utilization* (sensitivity to temporally extended
   environmental correlations beyond Markovian sufficiency), and (iii) *stability* under
   non-Markovian, temporally correlated perturbations. These are proposed as a minimal coupled
   triad: dropping any one yields a characteristic failure mode.

3. **Two case studies / engagement with recent literature** (§§6–7):
   - Aronowitz (2025) on semanticization: snapshot isolation strategies for episodic memory
     fail under diachronic processes; directly analogous to the snapshot vs. film distinction in
     AME. The persistence-entitlement check requires identifying an "intervention-sensitive
     stabilization pathway" that keeps a mapping intact as environmental correlation structure
     changes.
   - Ross (2021) on mechanisms vs. pathways: explanatory success can be carried by different
     causal vehicles (mechanism, pathway map, simulation, robustness); success does not
     automatically specify the stability conditions of its own bearer.

4. **Defence against three objections** (§5.6): redundancy with autonomy/agency theories
   (reply: AME is a test for entitlement, not a theory of how persistence occurs); category
   mistake (reply: explicit idealisation analysis is a well-established methodology); over-strength
   (reply: the audit is graded and context-sensitive; Markovian approximations remain legitimate
   with explicit scope conditions).

## Key Findings

**The isolation bias**: Mechanistic explanation, ABM, and robustness analysis share a structural
tendency to presuppose that the systems they describe persist long enough for their explanatory
content to matter. None provides a criterion for physical realizability under open-system dynamics
where persistence depends on active coupling management rather than insulation.

**AME as a meta-methodological audit**: AME shifts methodological attention from internal
sufficiency (is the model well-specified internally?) to temporal viability (can the described system
survive its environment?). It operates *prior to* and in *support of* the standard trio.

**Three coupled audit dimensions**:
- *Alignment*: a system that reproduces correct behavior only under temporally scrambled or
  memoryless environments fails the alignment audit — explanatory traction is present but
  persistence-entitlement is not.
- *Memory utilization*: viability requires sensitivity to past environmental states in ways
  irreducible to local statistics. This is substrate-neutral: no specific implementation is required,
  only that the model be compatible with such sensitivity.
- *Stability*: persistence under non-Markovian (coloured, delay-structured) perturbations, not
  merely under whitened noise or parameter variation. A model robust to whitened perturbations
  may still fail under correlated environmental fluctuations.

**Film vs. snapshot distinction**: Persistence is a property of system-environment *trajectories*,
not of configurations. A model that succeeds in a time-slice may succeed only because its
variables happen to align with a transient environmental correlation structure; explanatory success
dissociates from persistence when the coupling changes.

**Entitlement discipline**: Synchronic (snapshot) success does not automatically license diachronic
(persistence) claims. Earning persistence-entitlement requires specifying an "intervention-sensitive
stabilization pathway" and subjecting it to three mechanism-breaking test types: (i) structure
destruction under conserved gross statistics (scramble correlation while preserving variance),
(ii) enforced detuning under comparable effort, (iii) coupling-channel disruption with preserved
energetics.

**Scope conditions rescue Markovian models**: AME does not forbid idealization. Coarse-grained
or Markovian models remain legitimate when deployed with explicit scope conditions specifying
the timescales and coupling regimes under which memory effects can be neglected.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to several design dimensions of the Science toolkit and the
`hypothesis:0007-working-model` patchwork:

1. **Persistence claims in the working model** (`hypothesis:0007-working-model`): the patchwork
   makes implicit persistence claims — patches persist as evidence accumulates; beliefs remain
   stable pending new evidence; the federation structure endures across projects. Rey's analysis
   raises the question of whether these claims have been *earned* by the design or merely
   presupposed. The patchwork's robustness evaluation (question:0013) has so far focused on
   structural invariance; AME requires the additional check that this invariance is maintained
   under temporally correlated perturbations (non-i.i.d. evidence streams, topic drift, citation
   waves).

2. **Robustness evaluation in the toolkit** (`question:0013-robustness-reproducibility-evaluation`):
   Freiesleben and colleagues define robustness as stability under interventions on a modifier,
   which maps to AME's stability dimension. But Rey's framing adds a critical constraint:
   standard robustness analysis (Wimsatt 2007) tests stability of descriptions under model/
   parameter perturbation, not stability of the described system under realistic temporally
   structured environmental perturbations. This is necessary-but-not-sufficient for persistence-
   entitlement. The toolkit's robustness representation should incorporate this distinction.

3. **Nomological machines and isolation bias** (`question:0036-nomological-machine-patch-enabling-conditions`):
   Rey explicitly invokes Cartwright's nomological machine concept (§2.4): the three standard
   pillars produce "law-like success" only within partially insulated arrangements, and AME
   asks when such insulation still licenses persistence-entitlement in open, dappled environments.
   This directly extends question:0036's concern about encoding enabling conditions: a patch's
   enabling conditions must now include not just the static domain-scope context but also the
   temporal structure of the environment (Markovian vs. non-Markovian coupling regime).

4. **Evidence stream temporal structure**: The toolkit currently treats evidence items as roughly
   independent (i.i.d. in time). Rey's analysis of environmental memory as a physical resource
   implies that evidence streams with temporal correlation structure (citation waves, topic
   drift in literature, sequential experiment batches in a single lab) may require explicit non-
   Markovian handling. Treating such streams as i.i.d. is an instance of the isolation bias AME
   targets.

5. **Dynamical causal structure** (`question:0028-dynamical-causal-structure-representation`):
   AME engages directly with open-system, non-Markovian dynamics as a physical basis for
   persistence. Rey's framework complements question:0028's concern about representing causal
   structure conditioned on interventions: persistence-entitlement requires not only that the
   causal structure be correctly identified at a snapshot but that there exists an identifiable
   stabilization pathway keeping the causal mapping intact under drift.

## Limitations

- The paper is a philosophical/methodological proposal without empirical demonstration. The
  supplementary materials that would contrast the audit under coloured vs. memoryless perturbations
  are mentioned but not included in the main text (§4.7).
- The AME framework is domain-neutral and substrate-independent by design; this means it
  cannot directly prescribe implementation choices for the Science toolkit without additional
  design work.
- The three audit dimensions (alignment, memory utilization, stability) are specified at the level
  of desiderata rather than algorithms. Operationalization in a knowledge-graph context requires
  additional translation work not supplied by the paper.
- The paper engages primarily with biological and cognitive science cases; the mapping to
  epistemic model management systems (like the Science toolkit) is the present note's
  contribution, not the paper's own claim.
- Philip Rey is identified as the author but no institutional affiliation is given in the PDF.
