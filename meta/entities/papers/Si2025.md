# Detecting Implicit Biases of Large Language Models with Bayesian Hypothesis Testing

- **Authors:** Shijing Si, Xiaoming Jiang, Qinliang Su, and Lawrence Carin
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal/Venue:** Scientific Reports
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-95825-x
- **BibTeX key:** Si2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

Si et al. reformulate implicit bias detection in LLMs as a hypothesis-testing problem and argue for Bayes factors as a way to quantify evidence for both bias and no-bias hypotheses [@Si2025].
The contribution is a direct bridge between LLM evaluation and Bayesian evidence semantics.

## Methods

The framework uses binary-choice questions with stereotypical and anti-stereotypical options.
LLM preferences are modeled as Bernoulli outcomes, with null hypothesis `pi = 0.5` for no preference unless a context-specific baseline is supplied.
The paper compares exact binomial tests and Bayesian Bayes-factor tests across datasets such as BBQ, CrowS-Pairs, and Winogender.

## Key Findings

The paper argues that exact binomial tests cannot distinguish no evidence of bias from evidence of no bias, while Bayes factors quantify support for both competing hypotheses [@Si2025].
It reports broadly consistent bias behavior across English and French CrowS-Pairs with subtle contextual differences.

## Relevance

Science should use Bayesian evidence semantics when evaluating LLM agents and scientific assistants.
Bias, reliability, and safety evaluations should distinguish "no evidence" from "evidence of absence."
This directly supports Q07 and the evidence payload schema.

## Limitations

Bias evaluation depends on benchmark design, prompt format, and social context.
Binary-choice settings simplify open-ended agent behavior.
Science should treat bias scores as one source-reliability dimension, not a complete validation certificate.
