# A Versatile Causal Discovery Framework to Allow Causally-Related Hidden Variables

- **Authors:** Xinshuai Dong et al.
- **Year:** 2023
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **DOI/URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11001
- **BibTeX key:** Dong2023
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

Dong et al. propose RLCD, a rank-based latent causal discovery framework that allows hidden variables to be causes, effects, mediators, or part of hierarchical latent structures [@Dong2023].
The paper is important for Science because it directly challenges the default causal-sufficiency assumption behind many graph-learning workflows.

## Methods

The paper studies latent linear causal models and uses rank constraints in covariance submatrices to infer latent structure.
It develops necessary and sufficient conditions for identifiable latent patterns and introduces a three-phase RLCD algorithm to locate hidden variables, infer cardinalities, and recover causal structure over observed and latent variables.
It evaluates on synthetic data and real-world personality data.

## Key Findings

The paper argues that rank information can reveal latent causal structure beyond what conditional independence alone can recover [@Dong2023].
Under stated graphical and rank-faithfulness conditions, RLCD asymptotically identifies the Markov equivalence class of the latent causal graph.
In finite samples, experiments support the method's ability to handle more flexible latent structures than prior approaches.

## Relevance

Science should represent whether a causal discovery result assumes causal sufficiency.
If hidden variables may exist, graph edges should carry latent-confounding and latent-mediator uncertainty rather than being promoted directly to causal propositions.
This also supplies H03 reason codes such as `causal-sufficiency-assumption` and `latent-variable-risk`.

## Limitations

The framework assumes linear latent causal models and rank faithfulness.
The recovered object may still be an equivalence class rather than a unique causal graph.
Real-world generalization beyond tested domains remains uncertain.
