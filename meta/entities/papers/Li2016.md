# A Survey on Truth Discovery

- **Authors:** Yaliang Li, Jing Gao, Chuishi Meng, Qi Li, Lu Su, Bo Zhao, Wei Fan, Jiawei Han
- **Year:** 2015 issue with DOI publication in 2016; metadata check tracked by task:t074
- **Journal:** ACM SIGKDD Explorations Newsletter
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/2897350.2897352
- **BibTeX key:** Li2016
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

Li et al. survey truth discovery as the task of resolving conflicts among multi-source noisy information by jointly estimating source reliability and object-level truths [@Li2016].
The paper's core contribution is a unifying taxonomy that compares truth discovery methods by input data, source reliability assumptions, object properties, claimed-value structure, and output format [@Li2016].
For Science, the survey is useful because it treats evidence aggregation as an iterative inference problem over claims, sources, dependencies, uncertainty, and latent truth rather than as simple majority voting [@Li2016].

## Methods

The paper defines truth discovery over objects, sources, source-provided values, identified truths, and source weights that represent the probability of a source providing trustworthy information [@Li2016].
It presents the general principle that reliable sources should receive higher weights when they often provide inferred truths, and claims supported by reliable sources should be more likely to be selected as truths [@Li2016].
It organizes methods into iterative methods, optimization-based methods, and probabilistic graphical model methods, noting that coordinate descent and probabilistic inference often produce iterative update rules [@Li2016].
The survey then classifies method design choices across duplicate observations, conflict-free objects, heterogeneous formats, input uncertainty, structured and unstructured data, streaming data, labeled truths, source consistency, source independence, source dependencies, fine-grained source reliability, object difficulty, object relations, data types, hierarchical values, multiple truths, unknown truths, labeling outputs, scoring outputs, evaluation, and explanation [@Li2016].
Representative algorithms compared include TruthFinder, AccuSim, AccuCopy, 2-Estimates, 3-Estimates, Investment, SSTF, LTM, GTM, Regular EM, LCA, Apollo-social, CRH, and CATD [@Li2016].

## Key Findings

Truth discovery differs from majority voting because it can break ties using source reliabilities and can identify minority-supported values when minority sources are more reliable [@Li2016].
Most truth discovery methods assume some form of source consistency, meaning a source has a similar probability of being correct across objects, but the survey emphasizes that this assumption may be too coarse for domain-specific or fine-grained reliability [@Li2016].
Source independence is a common simplifying assumption, yet the survey highlights copying, shared extraction rules, source correlation, follower-retweet relations, and group-level dependencies as important failure modes for naive aggregation [@Li2016].
Input uncertainty is central when observations are extracted from text or supplied with confidence indicators, because uncertainty can arise from both content sources and extraction systems [@Li2016].
Output design matters because labeling produces directly usable truths while scoring preserves probabilities for candidate values and supports thresholding or downstream uncertainty handling [@Li2016].
The paper identifies unresolved challenges around unstructured data, object relations, source-reliability initialization, model selection, theoretical convergence and error bounds, efficiency, and performance evaluation without ground truth [@Li2016].

## Relevance

Truth discovery maps directly onto Science's evidence aggregation problem: multiple sources or studies may make incompatible claims about the same target, and aggregation should update both claim credibility and source reliability [@Li2016].
The survey supports an evidence payload schema that explicitly records comparison target, prior or model assumptions, heterogeneity, bias, diagnostics, estimand, and aggregation operator because truth discovery outcomes depend strongly on these modeling choices [@Li2016].
The source-dependency discussion is especially relevant for research-agent workflows, where apparent corroboration may reflect copying, shared datasets, shared extraction tools, or citation cascades rather than independent evidence [@Li2016].
The distinction between labeling and scoring supports continuous Bayesian belief states in Science because downstream reasoning should usually consume claim probabilities or credibility scores rather than only selected truths [@Li2016].
The paper's treatment of object relations and hierarchical values is relevant to causal graph construction because claims can constrain one another through entity relations, measurement hierarchies, temporal updates, or causal dependencies [@Li2016].

## Limitations

The paper is a survey rather than a benchmark study, so its comparisons are mostly conceptual and tabular rather than based on a uniform experimental protocol [@Li2016].
Many surveyed approaches rely on source consistency, source independence, object independence, or availability of ground truth for evaluation, all of which may fail in scientific evidence graphs [@Li2016].
The paper notes that most existing methods are batch algorithms and may be inefficient for streaming or large-scale truth discovery without specialized updates [@Li2016].
The survey does not provide a single decision procedure for choosing among methods, and it identifies model selection itself as an open problem [@Li2016].
The paper discusses explanation as important but observes that many methods output opaque intermediate scores that are hard for end-users and developers to interpret [@Li2016].

## Model / Tool Availability

The paper does not release a standalone truth discovery tool or implementation [@Li2016].
It surveys reusable algorithm families and points readers to original method papers for implementation details [@Li2016].

## Follow-up

Represent source dependency as explicit graph structure so Science can distinguish independent corroboration from copied or shared-provenance support.
Add aggregation metadata for cardinality, truth-existence assumptions, and output type so claim scorers know whether they are producing labels, probabilities, multiple truths, or unknown-truth states.
Compare truth discovery operators such as CRH, CATD, LTM, and LCA against Science evidence payloads where sources have heterogeneous formats, sparse observations, and correlated extraction pipelines.
