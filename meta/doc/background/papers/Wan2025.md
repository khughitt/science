# Large Language Models for Causal Discovery: Current Landscape and Future Directions

- **Authors:** Guangya Wan, Yunsheng Lu, Yuqi Wu, Mengxuan Hu, and Sheng Li
- **Year:** 2025
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **DOI/URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11068
- **BibTeX key:** Wan2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

Wan et al. survey how LLMs are being used for causal discovery through direct inference, post-hoc refinement, and prior-knowledge integration [@Wan2025].
The paper provides a useful taxonomy for Science's treatment of LLM-generated causal evidence.

## Methods

The survey reviews causal discovery background, LLM roles in causal discovery, evaluation datasets and metrics, applications, limitations, and future directions.
It distinguishes pairwise causal order prediction from full graph discovery and discusses metrics such as structural Hamming distance and normalized Hamming distance.

## Key Findings

LLMs can act as scalable but imperfect domain experts [@Wan2025].
They can propose causal graphs directly, refine statistical causal discovery outputs, or provide prior knowledge to guide statistical methods.
The survey emphasizes that evaluation remains difficult and that LLMs should not be treated as reliable standalone causal discovery systems.

## Relevance

Science should type LLM causal contributions by role: `direct_inference`, `prior_constraint`, `post_refinement`, `variable_proposal`, or `explanation`.
Each role has different evidential semantics.
This paper also supports recording prompts, variable descriptions, model identity, retrieval context, and benchmark/task type.

## Limitations

As a survey, it does not validate one implementation.
The field is fast-moving and metadata may change.
It covers many studies whose claims need primary-source checks before implementation decisions.

## Model / Tool Availability

No single software artifact is attached to the survey.
